Clinics vs. search demand

Reading clinic supply against consumer search interest side by side.

  • Botox leads on both supply and demand, unlike Atlanta and Charlotte where fillers out-supplied Botox. Botox / Neuromodulators is both the most-offered category by distinct clinic count (55 of 77 clinics) and the runaway search leader (index 83). Dermal / Lip Fillers is the second most-offered category (49 clinics) but searches at just 15 — Miami searchers likely split filler searches across brand names (Juvéderm, Restylane, Sculptra) rather than the generic term, so true filler-category demand is probably understated here, the same pattern seen in Atlanta.
  • Microneedling and Laser Hair Removal are the best-aligned categories. Both post solid search indexes (34 and 31) alongside strong clinic counts (41 and 40 — the 3rd and 4th most-offered categories), suggesting healthy alignment between what's searched and what's offered.
  • Emsculpt / Body Contouring is the starkest supply/demand mismatch in this dataset. It's the 5th most-offered category by clinic count (34 of 77 clinics) yet its search index is just 2 — among the lowest in the whole set. Miami searchers may be using more specific brand or device names ("body sculpting near me," "fat freezing," "Emsculpt Neo") rather than the generic term used here.
  • PRP Therapy and IPL / Photofacial both round to a search index of 0 despite real clinic supply (27 and 23 clinics respectively, the 6th and 8th most-offered categories). As with Emsculpt, this likely reflects search-term fragmentation (PRP facials, PRF, exosome treatments; BBL, Lumecca) more than an absence of real consumer interest.
  • Sculptra / Biostimulators stands out as comparatively demand-forward. With only 11 of 77 clinics offering it (tied for 10th most-offered), it still posts a search index of 11 — higher than several categories with 2–3x its clinic supply (Emsculpt, PRP, Chemical Peels). That's a rare case in this dataset where demand appears to be running ahead of clinic supply rather than behind it.
  • PDO Thread Lift and CoolSculpting/CoolTone are the rarest categories in the Miami dataset — 11 and 5 of 77 clinics offer them, respectively (PDO Thread Lift search index: 0; CoolSculpting: 1). Both remain niche, low-adoption categories across this market.

Are we comparing the same thing?

Short answer: not always — and that's worth knowing before reading too much into any single price range above.

  • Botox's published per-unit pricing is thin, and much of what looks like pricing may not be clinic-specific at all. The exact phrase — "$12–$15 per unit" — turned up identically, word-for-word, across 7 unrelated Miami clinics, which reads far more like a generic industry-average figure surfacing in search results than each clinic's own posted rate. Those clinics are marked "Partial" rather than "Yes" for that reason. Only a couple of clinics (Pler Clinics at $9.99/unit, South Florida Face and Body at $12/unit) show pricing specific enough to trust directly.
  • Dermal / Lip Fillers ($650–$785/syringe) is one of the thinnest genuine price samples in this report. Only 2 of 55 tracked filler listings had a real, clinic-specific price; the same generic-range contamination pattern seen in Botox pricing (repeated "$600–$1,200" and "$600–$2,000" text) affected several more listings. Click "Dermal / Lip Fillers" above to see the exact wording for every listing.
  • Microneedling / RF ($284–$999) mixes basic microneedling with RF-enhanced Morpheus8 treatments. Basic microneedling/dermaplaning facials price around $284 (Facial Mania's two Miami locations); Morpheus8 at Monaco MedSpa runs $699–$999/session — a meaningfully more involved treatment under the same broad category label.
  • Laser Hair Removal ($40–$330) blends single-session and multi-session package pricing. Some clinics quote a per-session starting price ($40–$50); Facial Mania's package price ($330 for 6 sessions) works out closer to $55/session. A one-off session price and a bundled package price aren't quite the same comparison, even under the same label.
  • What we did about it: every "# clinics" figure in this report counts distinct clinics, not raw price listings (a clinic offering both Botox and Dysport only counts once under "Botox / Neuromodulators"). We can't fully normalize prices to a common unit automatically, since clinics describe quantities and package sizes in inconsistent free text, so instead every procedure name is clickable and opens the full list of underlying listings — exact wording, exact price, per clinic — so you can check whether two prices are actually for the same thing before comparing them.
  • Not every listing has a price at all — and Miami is thinner on published pricing than Charlotte or Atlanta. Just 30 of 77 Miami clinics (39%) show any pricing at all, full or partial, versus 70% in Charlotte and 32% in Atlanta. Most Miami med spas lean on "book a free consultation" rather than a published rate card, and where a number does appear, it's disproportionately likely to be a one-time Groupon promotion rather than a standing price.
