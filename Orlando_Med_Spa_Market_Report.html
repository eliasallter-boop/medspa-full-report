Relative search interest for each treatment among Orlando–area searchers over the past 12 months, indexed 0–100 against the single highest-volume term (Botox) across all comparisons. One representative search term was used per category — see note below.

Source: trends.google.com, geo = Orlando-Daytona Beach-Melbourne, FL (Nielsen DMA US-FL-534), Aug 2025–Aug 2026, weekly values averaged and read directly from the Trends API response rather than estimated visually, using a single Chrome tab with spaced-out requests (no parallel tabs) to avoid the bot-detection block hit in an earlier attempt for this city. Google Trends indexes relative search volume for the exact term entered, not category-wide demand — e.g. "Dermal Fillers" doesn't capture searches for "Juvéderm" or "Restylane," and "IPL Photofacial" doesn't capture "BBL" or "Lumecca." Treat this as a directional signal, not an absolute demand figure.

Clinics vs. search demand

Reading clinic supply against consumer search interest side by side.

  • Botox leads both supply and demand. Botox / Neuromodulators is both the most-offered category by distinct clinic count (50 of 71 clinics, 70%) and the runaway search leader (index 80). Dermal / Lip Fillers is the second most-offered category (41 clinics, 58%) but searches at essentially index 1 — Orlando searchers likely split filler searches across brand names (Juvéderm, Restylane, Sculptra) rather than the generic term, the same understatement pattern seen in every city in this project.
  • Microneedling and Laser Hair Removal are the best-aligned categories. Both are tied for 3rd-4th most-offered (19 clinics each, 27%) and post the two highest non-Botox search indexes (31 and 29) — real consumer interest that's actually matched by real clinic supply, a rarer pattern in this dataset.
  • Medical Weight Loss / GLP-1 is the starkest demand-ahead-of-supply mismatch. Only 10 of 71 clinics offer it (14%, tied for 6th-7th) yet it posts a search index of 51 — reflecting the broader Semaglutide/GLP-1 wave outpacing local clinic listings, a pattern that turned out to be even sharper in Dallas and Houston once those cities' Trends data came in.
  • Body Contouring / CoolSculpting is the starkest supply-ahead-of-demand mismatch. It's the 5th most-offered category (15 clinics, 21%) yet its search index is just 1 — among the lowest in the whole set, consistent with the near-zero CoolSculpting-brand search interest seen in every city researched.
  • Kybella and IPL / Photofacial are thin or invisible on the demand side despite real supply. Kybella sits at just 2 of 71 clinics (3%) and a search index of 0; IPL / Photofacial has moderate supply (7 clinics, 10%) but an index of 0 — searchers may be using device or brand names (BBL, Lumecca) rather than the generic category term.

Are we comparing the same thing?

Short answer: not always — and that's worth knowing before reading too much into any single price range above.

  • Botox's published per-unit pricing comes from just 13 of 50 clinics, most of it introductory or event-day promo pricing rather than a standing rate (Couture Med Spa's $7.97/unit intro rate appears identically across its Oviedo, Kissimmee, and Lake Mary locations, since all three are the same chain). 3 unrelated clinics (VIO Med Spa SoDo, 180 MedSpa, Windermere Medical Spa & Laser Institute - Winter Park) show the exact same '$12-$15/unit' phrasing seen repeated verbatim in Atlanta and Miami — treated as generic boilerplate estimate text, not real clinic pricing, and excluded from this report's range.
  • Dermal / Lip Fillers ($349–$850/syringe) mixes single-syringe rates, member rates, and multi-syringe packages — not all directly comparable to each other. The same 3-clinic boilerplate contamination pattern appears here too ('$600-$1,200/syringe', verbatim across VIO SoDo, 180 MedSpa, and Windermere Winter Park) and was excluded.
  • Microneedling (19 clinics) has almost no genuine pricing — only 1 of 19 publishes a real clinic price. A second apparent listing (Homa Rejuvenation Center's $880-$1,650/session) came from a third-party pricing-aggregator site rather than the clinic's own website and was excluded as not clinic-verified.
  • Skin Tightening ($229–$2,400) is a single clinic's two very different treatments — an RF session versus a full non-surgical facelift package — not a market range at all; treat this figure with caution.
  • What we did about it: every "# clinics" figure in this report counts distinct clinics, not raw price listings. We can't fully normalize prices to a common unit automatically, since clinics describe quantities and package sizes in inconsistent free text, so every procedure name is clickable and opens the full list of underlying listings — exact wording, exact price, per clinic — so you can check whether two prices are actually for the same thing before comparing them.
  • 65% of Orlando clinics show any pricing at all, full or partial — the highest rate seen in this project after Charlotte (70%), well above Atlanta and Miami (32%) and West Palm Beach (31%). The Oviedo/Winter Springs/Lake Mary/Sanford corridor, dense with independent single-location clinics running visible promo pricing, drives most of this.
