Clinics vs. search demand

Reading clinic supply against consumer search interest side by side.

  • Botox leads on both supply and demand, by a wide margin. It's the single most-offered category by distinct clinic count (34 of 43 clinics — nearly 4 in 5) and the runaway leader on search, with an index of 80 — more than double the next-closest term (Microneedling, 36). Charlotte search demand is heavily concentrated on Botox specifically, consistent with it being the treatment nearly every clinic leads with in ads and homepage copy.
  • Fillers are a close #2 on supply but far behind on search — with the same caveat seen elsewhere. Dermal/lip fillers are offered by 29 of 43 clinics, but the generic term "Lip Filler" indexes at just 19. This is probably understated: Charlotte searchers likely split filler searches across brand names (Juvéderm, Restylane, Sculptra, Versa) rather than the generic term, so true category demand is likely higher than this single term suggests.
  • Microneedling and Laser Hair Removal look well-matched. Both post the next-highest search indexes (36 and 32) alongside solid clinic counts (20 and 13), suggesting healthy alignment between what's searched and what's offered.
  • Chemical Peels are a supply-heavy outlier. 21 of 43 clinics offer peels — the second-most-common category after Botox — yet the search index sits at just 12, well behind Microneedling and Laser Hair Removal despite similar or greater clinic supply. Peels may be sold more as an add-on to a facial than something clients actively search for by name.
  • Kybella shows the sharpest supply/demand gap in the dataset. It's offered by 13 of 43 clinics — tied with Laser Hair Removal for clinic count — but its search index is just 4, among the lowest measured. CoolSculpting, Emsculpt, Ultherapy, and PDO Thread Lift show the same pattern (index 4 despite real clinic supply), suggesting either different search terms are in play locally (e.g. "double chin treatment," "fat freezing near me") or clinic supply here is running well ahead of measurable local search demand.
  • Ultherapy is nearly absent from the Charlotte market — only 1 of 43 clinics offers it, versus a much larger presence in bigger, higher-price markets like Manhattan. That's likely a market-size and price-point story: Ultherapy is a premium device investment, and Charlotte's mostly independent, smaller-footprint med spas appear to favor lower-cost RF/laser skin-tightening alternatives (Morpheus8, Virtue RF, HALO) instead.

Are we comparing the same thing?

Short answer: not always — and that's worth knowing before reading too much into any single price range above.

  • Botox's $4–$1,200 range mixes per-unit rates with one very different treatment. Most of the spread ($4–$15) is ordinary per-unit pricing variation across clinics. The $1,200 figure is Capizzi MD's "Stop Sweating" (hyperhidrosis) underarm Botox, priced per vial for a much larger dose than a standard forehead or crow's-feet treatment — not a "premium" cosmetic Botox price. Click "Botox / Neuromodulators" above to see the exact wording for every listing.
  • Emsculpt / Body Contouring ($3,500–$13,500) is almost entirely a package-size story. Nearly every priced listing in this category is actually a non-surgical Sculptra-based butt-lift or breast-lift package (Lifted Aesthetics), priced by vial count — 10, 20, or 30 vials. It's a legitimate category grouping (Manhattan's report groups it the same way), but the number has little to do with the muscle-stimulation device "Emsculpt" itself, which none of the tracked Charlotte clinics currently publish a price for.
  • Dermal/Lip Fillers ($150–$10,000) spans ordinary syringes and one specialty body procedure. The vast majority of listings fall in the $450–$1,540 range for standard face/lip filler syringes. The $10,000 high end is Capizzi MD's Alloclae gluteal/hip-dip filler — a fundamentally different, much larger-volume body procedure that happens to use the word "filler."
  • Laser Hair Removal ($50–$6,000) is a package-size problem, not a pricing problem. $50 is realistic for a single small area (lip, underarms); $6,000 is realistic for a full-body, 7-session package (Lifted Aesthetics). Both are legitimately "Laser Hair Removal," just at opposite ends of scope.
  • What we did about it: every "# clinics" figure in this report counts distinct clinics, not raw price listings (a clinic offering both Botox and Dysport only counts once under "Botox / Neuromodulators"). We can't fully normalize prices to a common unit automatically, since clinics describe quantities and package sizes in inconsistent free text, so instead every procedure name is clickable and opens the full list of underlying listings — exact wording, exact price, per clinic — so you can check whether two prices are actually for the same thing before comparing them.
  • Not every listing has a price at all. Many Charlotte clinics require a consultation before quoting, especially for injectables and body-contouring — so a category's price range is often built from a minority of its listings (for example, only 3 of 12 tracked IPL/Photofacial listings had a published price). A real range, just not a complete one.
