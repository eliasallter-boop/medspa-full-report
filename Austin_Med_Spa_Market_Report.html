Coverage at a glance

Since a single citywide Google Maps search consistently capped at 6 visible results, coverage was built from 15 neighborhood/suburb-scoped searches instead across the Austin metro (Downtown, South Congress, Westlake, The Domain, Round Rock, Cedar Park, Georgetown, Pflugerville, Lakeway, Bee Cave, Circle C, East Austin, Mueller, Hyde Park, and Buda), turning up 107 raw results (103 logged as trackable candidates after de-duplication and 4 pre-exclusions). 3 more were dropped as non-medical spas during research (IV/wellness-only businesses with no injectables or lasers) and 2 were unverifiable (no matching independent business found) — leaving 98 confirmed medical spas.

Relative search interest for each treatment among Austin–area searchers over the past 12 months, indexed 0–100 against the single highest-volume term (Botox) across all comparisons. One representative search term was used per category — see note below.

Source: trends.google.com, geo = Austin, TX (Nielsen DMA US-TX-635), Aug 2025–Aug 2026, weekly values averaged and read directly from the Trends API response rather than estimated visually, using a single Chrome tab with spaced-out requests (no parallel tabs). Google Trends indexes relative search volume for the exact term entered, not category-wide demand — e.g. "Dermal Fillers" doesn't capture searches for "Juvéderm" or "Restylane," and "IPL Photofacial" doesn't capture "BBL" or "Lumecca." Treat this as a directional signal, not an absolute demand figure.

Clinics vs. search demand

Reading clinic supply against consumer search interest side by side.

  • Botox's supply lead is the widest seen in this project. 87 of 98 clinics (89%, easily the highest percentage of any city researched) offer it, and it's also the runaway search leader (index 75). Dermal / Lip Fillers is a very close second in supply (76 clinics, 78%) but searches at essentially zero (index 0) — the most extreme fillers-search-understatement found anywhere in this project.
  • Laser Hair Removal shows a real demand-ahead-of-supply gap. It's tied for 7th-8th most-offered (28 clinics, 29%) yet posts the second-highest search index in the city (39). Microneedling is better matched on both sides (3rd supply at 40 clinics/41%, and the highest non-Botox demand at 33).
  • Medical Weight Loss / GLP-1 is again a sharp demand-ahead-of-supply mismatch. Only 17 of 98 clinics offer it (17%, tied 9th-10th) yet it posts a search index of 47, the second-highest non-Botox demand figure in the city.
  • Chemical Peels is a notable supply-ahead-of-demand mismatch. It's the 4th most-offered category (38 clinics, 39%) yet its search index is just 3.
  • Body Contouring / CoolSculpting, Kybella, PRP / PRF, and Skin Tightening all post a search index of essentially 0 despite real clinic supply (26, 17, 16, and 13 clinics respectively) — Austin searchers appear to lean heavily on Botox, fillers, laser hair removal, microneedling, and GLP-1 terms, with almost no generic search volume for the rest of the category list.

Are we comparing the same thing?

Short answer: not always — and that's worth knowing before reading too much into any single price range above.

  • Botox's published per-unit pricing comes from 16 of 87 clinics — a wider genuine sample than most prior cities, ranging from Results Weight Loss & Med Spa's $4.17/unit up to SkinSpirit Austin's $20/unit ceiling. 7 unrelated clinics (Face to Face Spa at Downtown Austin, Austin Aesthetics Club, Alchemy Wellness Med Spa, ULALA Med Spa) show the exact same '$12-$15/unit' phrasing seen repeated verbatim in every prior city of this project — treated as generic boilerplate estimate text and excluded from this report's range. A second aggregator pattern ('$200-$900/session,' matching the Dallas/Houston figures) appeared at 3 more clinics and was also excluded.
  • Dermal / Lip Fillers ($250–$1,200/syringe) comes from 8 of 76 clinics spanning very different products (a $250 lip flip vs. a $1,200 Juvederm Voluma syringe are not the same treatment). One outlier, Ginger Allure MedSpa, prices filler 'from $5/unit' — a per-unit structure that isn't directly comparable to the per-syringe figures above and was excluded from the range. The same boilerplate ('$600-$1,200/syringe') and aggregator ('$600-$2,000/syringe') contamination patterns appear here too, at 6 clinics, and were excluded.
  • Laser Hair Removal ($40/session–$3,750) spans three incompatible billing models — a single-session rate, a 30-minute unlimited-area promo, and multi-month unlimited packages priced from $325/mo up to $3,750 for 18 months. These are not the same purchase and shouldn't be read as one market rate.
  • What we did about it: every "# clinics" figure in this report counts distinct clinics, not raw price listings. We can't fully normalize prices to a common unit automatically, since clinics describe quantities and package sizes in inconsistent free text, so every procedure name is clickable and opens the full list of underlying listings — exact wording, exact price, per clinic — so you can check whether two prices are actually for the same thing before comparing them.
  • 32% of Austin clinics show any pricing at all, full or partial — on the lower end of the range seen in this project so far, tied with Atlanta and Miami (both 32%) and just above West Palm Beach (31%), well below Charlotte (70%), Orlando (65%), and Houston (56%). Austin's genuine pricing that does exist skews toward flat-fee and membership models rather than simple per-unit rates.
