Clinics vs. search demand

Reading clinic supply against consumer search interest side by side.

  • Botox leads on both supply and demand. It's now the single most-offered category by distinct clinic count (41 of 176 clinics, just ahead of fillers at 40) and the runaway leader on search, with an index of 81 — more than double the next-closest term (Laser Hair Removal, 33). Search demand in NY is heavily concentrated on Botox specifically, which likely explains why it's the treatment clinics lead with in ads and homepage copy.
  • Fillers are a close #2 on supply but far behind on search — with a caveat. Dermal/lip fillers are offered by nearly as many clinics as Botox (40 vs. 41) but the generic term "Lip Filler" indexes at just 13. This is probably understated: NY searchers likely split filler searches across brand names (Juvederm, Restylane, Sculptra) rather than the generic term, so true category demand is likely higher than this single term suggests.
  • Laser Hair Removal and Microneedling look well-matched. Both post the next-highest search indexes (33 and 29) alongside solid mid-tier clinic counts (27 and 35), suggesting healthy alignment between what's searched and what's offered.
  • Body-contouring devices show a supply/demand gap. CoolSculpting, Emsculpt, Ultherapy, and Kybella each sit at a search index of 2–3 — among the lowest measured — despite each being offered by 10–17 clinics. Either NY searchers are using different terms for these (e.g. "fat freezing near me"), or clinic supply here is running ahead of measurable search demand.
  • PDO Thread Lift and IPL/Photofacial are niche on both sides — modest clinic counts (16 and 24) and negligible NY search volume (index 1) — with no meaningful mismatch to flag.

Are we comparing the same thing?

Short answer: not always — and that's worth knowing before reading too much into any single price range above.

  • The clearest example is Botox. Its $5–$650 range mixes two different pricing models: some clinics quote a flat price for a fixed unit count (e.g. "20 units for $199"), others quote a per-unit rate (e.g. "$10/unit," which would be $200 for the same 20 units, or far less for a smaller touch-up). A $5 line item and a $650 line item aren't necessarily "cheap" vs. "expensive" — they may just be priced by different units entirely. Click "Botox / Neuromodulators" above to see the exact wording clinics used for each price.
  • Laser Hair Removal ($39–$7,000) is a package-size problem, not a pricing problem. $39 is a realistic price for a single small area (upper lip, underarms); $7,000 is a realistic price for a full-body, multi-session package. Both are legitimately "Laser Hair Removal," just at opposite ends of scope.
  • Fillers and Sculptra span single-unit vs. multi-unit purchases (one syringe or vial vs. a full treatment plan requiring several), which is most of why those ranges look so wide.
  • What we did about it: every "# clinics" figure in this report now counts distinct clinics, not raw price listings (a clinic offering both Botox and Dysport used to get counted twice under "Botox" — it no longer does). We can't fully normalize prices to a common unit automatically, since clinics describe quantities in inconsistent free text, so instead every procedure name is clickable and opens the full list of underlying listings — exact wording, exact price, per clinic — so you can check whether two prices are actually for the same thing before comparing them.
  • Not every listing has a price at all. Many clinics require a consultation before quoting, so a category's price range is often built from a minority of its listings (noted under each row in the table above) — a real range, just not a complete one.
