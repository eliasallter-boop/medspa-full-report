Coverage at a glance

This corridor is a chain of ten small, adjoining municipalities rather than one city, so coverage was built from 10 municipality-scoped searches (Jersey City, Hoboken, Weehawken, Union City, West New York, Secaucus, Fort Lee, Edgewater, Englewood/Englewood Cliffs, and Teaneck) instead of a single citywide pull. That turned up 32 confirmed medical spas with a real, independent storefront in one of these towns. Three of the ten towns searched — Union City, West New York, and Secaucus — returned no standalone med spa with a confirmed address inside their own borders; residents there appear to be served mainly by neighboring Hoboken, Jersey City, and North Bergen clinics rather than by any dedicated local storefront — a real finding, not a research gap (see "Geographic coverage" below).

Google Trends geo-targets at the DMA level, and Hudson & Bergen counties, NJ fall within the same New York DMA as Manhattan — not their own New Jersey DMA — so these figures are the identical regional index already used in the Manhattan report (its Botox figure of 81 matches exactly). Google Trends indexes relative search volume for the exact term entered, not category-wide demand — e.g. "Lip Filler" doesn't capture searches for "Juvéderm" or "Restylane," and "Morpheus8" doesn't capture generic "RF skin tightening." Treat this as a directional signal, not an absolute demand figure.

Clinics vs. search demand

Reading clinic supply against consumer search interest side by side.

  • Botox's supply lead tracks its search dominance closely. 27 of 32 clinics (84%) offer it, and it's also the runaway search leader in the New York DMA (index 81, by far the highest of any term compared). Dermal / Lip Fillers is a close second in supply (26 clinics, 81%) but searches far behind (index 13) — a supply-ahead-of-demand gap seen in almost every city in this project.
  • Medical Weight Loss / GLP-1 is the widest demand-ahead-of-supply mismatch found in this metro. Only 4 of 32 clinics (13%) offer it, yet "Semaglutide" posts a search index of 34 — the second-highest figure in the corridor, behind only Botox.
  • Laser Hair Removal is comparatively well matched. It's the 3rd most-offered category (15 clinics, 47%) and also the 3rd-highest search term (index 33).
  • Sculptra / Biostimulators and Kybella show real search interest (index 7 and 2) with zero confirmed local supply in this dataset — no clinic among the 32 researched publishes either treatment on its own service pages. That may understate true availability, since this pass relied on public website and directory listings rather than a call-in check of every clinic's full injectable menu, but it's a genuine gap in what's publicly advertised.
  • Skin Tightening (Morpheus8/RF/Ultherapy), PRP/PRF, and Body Contouring all post modest clinic supply (6, 8, and 3 clinics respectively) against very low search interest (index 2, 3, and 3) — consistent with this project's recurring pattern of body-contouring and regenerative-treatment categories under-indexing on generic search terms relative to injectables.

Are we comparing the same thing?

Short answer: rarely, in this metro — pricing transparency here is the lowest seen in this project so far.

  • Botox's published per-unit pricing comes from only 3 of 27 clinics — fillir, Hoboken ($14/unit Botox/Dysport/Xeomin), Evolve Med Spa's Jersey City and Hoboken locations ($11/unit Botox/Dysport, with membership tiers as low as $9/unit Xeomin), and The Hudson Med Spa, Englewood ($150 minimum per session, covering roughly 10 Botox or 30 Dysport units — an effective per-unit rate at the higher end of this range for small treatment areas). The other 24 clinics offering Botox/neuromodulators in this dataset do not publish pricing.
  • Dermal / Lip Fillers ($320–$800/syringe) comes from just 1 of 26 clinics with a clean per-syringe figure — Evolve Med Spa's Lip Membership rate ($500/syringe across all lip filler brands). A separate 3–5 syringe facial-filler promotion (from $1,600 total, i.e. roughly $320–$533/syringe blended) was folded into the low end of this range since it's priced as a multi-syringe package rather than a single clean rate, not a directly comparable figure. The other 25 clinics offering fillers in this dataset do not publish pricing.
  • Laser Hair Removal ($350/session–$1,300, 6-session package) comes from a single source — The Hudson Med Spa, Englewood, which bundles underarm + bikini line laser hair removal at $350/session (regular $1,950 for a 6-session package, promotional $1,300 for the same package). The other 14 clinics offering laser hair removal in this dataset do not publish pricing, and a single clinic's promo rate shouldn't be read as a metro-wide market rate.
  • Skin Tightening ($500–$3,500) comes from one source with a wide, area-dependent range — Marina Medispa, Edgewater's fractional laser skin resurfacing, priced by treatment area rather than a flat rate. Not a single comparable number.
  • What we did about it: every "# clinics" figure in this report counts distinct clinics, not raw price listings, and every procedure name is clickable so you can see exactly which clinic and listing a number came from before comparing prices across clinics.
  • 16% of clinics in this dataset show any pricing at all, full or partial — the lowest figure in this project so far, well below Charlotte (70%), Orlando (65%), Houston (56%), and even Austin/Atlanta/Miami/West Palm Beach (31–33%). This dense, expensive, PATH-and-ferry-commuter corridor skews heavily toward consult-first, price-on-request storefronts rather than published rate cards — a genuinely different pattern from every other city researched in this project.

Worth re-checking with a phone-book/permit-registry pass rather than web search alone before concluding these towns are truly unserved.
