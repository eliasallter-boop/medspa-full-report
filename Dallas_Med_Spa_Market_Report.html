Search interest in Dallas (Google Trends)

Relative search interest for each treatment among Dallas–area searchers over the past 12 months, indexed 0–100 against the single highest-volume term (Botox) across all comparisons. One representative search term was used per category — see note below.

Source: trends.google.com, geo = Dallas-Fort Worth, TX (Nielsen DMA US-TX-623), Aug 2025–Aug 2026, weekly values averaged and read directly from the Trends API response rather than estimated visually, using a single Chrome tab with spaced-out requests (no parallel tabs). Google Trends indexes relative search volume for the exact term entered, not category-wide demand — e.g. "Dermal Fillers" doesn't capture searches for "Juvéderm" or "Restylane," and "IPL Photofacial" doesn't capture "BBL" or "Lumecca." Treat this as a directional signal, not an absolute demand figure.

Clinics vs. search demand

Reading clinic supply against consumer search interest side by side.

  • Botox leads both supply and demand. Botox / Neuromodulators is the most-offered category (66 of 101 clinics, 65%) and the runaway search leader (index 79). Dermal / Lip Fillers is a close second in supply (55 clinics, 54%) but searches at just index 2 — the same generic-term understatement seen everywhere else in this project.
  • Microneedling and Laser Hair Removal are the best-aligned categories again. Both are 4th and 5th most-offered (32 clinics/32% and 26 clinics/26%) and post the two highest non-Botox search indexes (35 and 36).
  • Medical Weight Loss / GLP-1 is the single starkest demand-ahead-of-supply mismatch found anywhere in this project. Only 14 of 101 clinics offer it (14%, 10th most-offered) yet it posts a search index of 60 — the highest GLP-1/weight-loss search figure across all 8 cities researched.
  • Body Contouring / CoolSculpting is the starkest supply-ahead-of-demand mismatch. It's the 3rd most-offered category (36 clinics, 36%) yet its search index is just 3, among the lowest in the whole set despite being offered by more than a third of Dallas clinics.
  • Kybella and Skin Tightening remain thin on both sides. Kybella sits at 7 of 101 clinics (7%) with an index of 2; Skin Tightening has 10 clinics (10%) and an index of 1 — both low-visibility categories, consistent with every other city in this project.

Are we comparing the same thing?

Short answer: not always — and that's worth knowing before reading too much into any single price range above.

  • Botox's published per-unit pricing comes from just 9 of 66 clinics, most of it introductory or first-time promo pricing rather than a standing rate. 10 unrelated clinics (It's A Secret Uptown, Revive Primary Care, The Science of Beauty, Modern Medical Spa, D'Allure Medspa, Calypso Aesthetic Lab, SkinSpirit Preston Royal, the L.A.B. med spa, Bella MedSpa, and VIO Med Spa Casa Linda) show the exact same '$12-$15/unit' phrasing seen repeated verbatim in every prior city of this project — treated as generic boilerplate estimate text, not real clinic pricing, and excluded from this report's range.
  • Dermal / Lip Fillers ($283–$799/syringe) mixes single-syringe rates, new-client rates, and Groupon package prices — not all directly comparable to each other. The same boilerplate contamination pattern appears here too ('$600-$1,200/syringe', verbatim across 8 clinics) and was excluded, as was a third-party-aggregator estimate from Elite Med Spa of Texas.
  • Laser Hair Removal (26 clinics) is priced on five different, incompatible structures — per-session, per-6-treatment-package, Groupon 3-treatment package, pay-per-visit, and monthly membership — so the $59–$700 range mostly reflects which structure a clinic uses, not which clinic is more or less expensive.
  • Skin Tightening ($429–$4,000) is really two clinics' two very different treatments — a single Morpheus8 session versus a full multi-visit EMFACE series — not a market range at all; treat this figure with caution.
  • What we did about it: every "# clinics" figure in this report counts distinct clinics, not raw price listings. We can't fully normalize prices to a common unit automatically, since clinics describe quantities and package sizes in inconsistent free text, so every procedure name is clickable and opens the full list of underlying listings — exact wording, exact price, per clinic — so you can check whether two prices are actually for the same thing before comparing them.
  • 55% of Dallas clinics show any pricing at all, full or partial — in the middle of the range seen in this project so far, below Charlotte (70%) and Orlando (highest among Florida cities), but above Atlanta and Miami (32%) and West Palm Beach (31%). McKinney and Richardson's dense cluster of independent, promo-driven clinics drives most of this.
